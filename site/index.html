why

you didn't become an engineer to babysit robots.

you’ve got more agents than you can keep track of.

five, ten of them at once. you hop from tab to tab all day, and by the evening your brain is soup.

you’d love to hand off more. but letting go is scary.

nobody tells you how to set up agents that run on their own. and when you try, you worry they’ll go off and do the wrong thing, with a token bill to match.

and none of it is fun!

it feels like working the counter at the post office.

what if your team looked like a video game?

bise

so here’s how bise does it.

you talk to one agent, bise. it starts an agent for each job, and only bothers you when something actually needs you.

it’s a simple setup. one lead, one agent per task. the lead figures out who does what, and in which order.

you’re still the boss. you choose the work, and what you want to look at yourself.

and it doesn’t cost more. they’re the same agents you’d run by hand, and bise itself is free: you only pay for the models, with the plan or the key you already have.

now

one agent to talk to.

bise sees all your projects. you say what’s on your mind and it passes the work to the right one. back from lunch? it’ll tell you what happened.

it feels a bit like playing the Sims.

each agent is a bim, a little character in its project’s office. you can see who’s typing, who’s stuck and who’s waiting on you. click one and you’re in its thread.

it only knocks when it needs you.

questions from every project land in one inbox, with one banner, once. you answer from wherever you are, and your answer stays next to the thread it came from.

artifacts

questions you can actually answer.

when ten agents are running, one asks « can i drop the old discount codes? ». which codes? you scroll back through a thread you haven't read in an hour to find out.

so when a decision needs you, the agent writes you a page. it shows what it did and the options side by side. you leave notes right on it, pick one, and send it all back in one go.

questions

you'll ask.

is it free?
yes. bise is free and open source. you pay for the models the way you already do: your ChatGPT Plus or Pro plan, a coding plan, OpenRouter, or an API key.

will it cost me more?
no. they're the same agents you'd run by hand. the lead only starts one when a job needs it, and does the small stuff itself. /usage shows what you spent today and this week, agent by agent, and /pause stops everything in one go.

is it safe to let them run?
the models are good at this now, and the people who get the most out of agents let them work. so by default, bise doesn't stop to ask. if you'd like a check, shift+tab turns on auto: safe commands just run, risky ones wait for your yes in the inbox, inside a macOS sandbox.

will one thread hold up on a project like mine?
yes. the lead doesn't carry your code. each agent reads what it needs with a fresh head, and the lead keeps the plan and the reports. bise is built with bise: one thread since September 28, 934 agents, 4,588 commits. it's still the same thread.

what does bise actually do for me?
it's your team. you say what you want. the lead splits it into jobs, starts the agents, keeps them out of each other's way, answers their small questions, remembers what you did weeks ago, and cleans up after them. you only hear about what needs you. a small software factory, and you're the boss.

i already use Claude Code or Codex.
keep your models, your MCP servers and your skills: bise brings them over. what changes is how you work: one lead to talk to, instead of seven sessions to watch.

which models?
the ones you already pay for: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Mistral and more, through a key, OpenRouter or your plan. the plans bise can use.

what does it run on?
bise desktop: Macs with Apple silicon, macOS 14 or later. bise terminal: macOS and Linux. you need git.

open source?
yes, under Apache-2.0. read it, fork it, send a :*.
